
# Machine Learning Collective - CS197 - Computer Vision B

**Learning computer vision through research, collaboration, and implementation.**

Welcome to the GitHub organization of **Computer Vision B**, a **Machine Learning Collective** study group working through **CS197** together. We explore computer vision research, break down research papers, discuss the ideas behind modern methods, and build practical understanding through experimentation and implementation.

## About Us

Computer Vision B is a collaborative learning group focused on understanding how machines interpret and reason about visual information.

As part of CS197, we study research papers, examine the problems they address, understand their technical contributions, and evaluate the evidence behind their results.

Our goal is not just to read papers, but to understand the ideas well enough to explain, question, and implement them.

## What We Do

- **Research Paper Reviews:** Break down computer vision papers, their motivation, methodology, experiments, and contributions.
- **Concept Exploration:** Build a strong understanding of the theories and techniques behind computer vision systems.
- **Practical Implementations:** Experiment with research ideas through code, notebooks, and reproducible implementations.
- **Group Discussions:** Share insights, challenge assumptions, and learn from one another.
- **Technical Presentations:** Communicate research findings clearly through presentations and group defenses.

## Current Focus: CS197

We are currently studying CS197 together, using its research-oriented approach to understand and critically evaluate advances in computer vision and related fields.

Our paper discussions generally follow this structure:

1. **Problem:** What problem is the paper trying to solve, and why does it matter?
2. **Prior Work:** What assumptions or limitations exist in previous approaches?
3. **Key Insight:** What new idea does the paper introduce?
4. **Technical Approach:** How does the proposed method work?
5. **Evidence:** How is the method evaluated, and what do the results demonstrate?
6. **Impact and Limitations:** What does the work change, and what questions remain?

## Repository Structure

As the group grows, repositories may be organized around individual research papers, implementations, experiments, and shared learning resources.

```text
computer-vision-b/
├── paper-reviews/       # Research paper summaries and analyses
├── implementations/    # Code implementations of research methods
├── experiments/        # Notebooks, evaluations, and experiments
├── presentations/      # Group presentations and defense materials
└── resources/          # Learning materials and useful references
```

Each project can maintain its own README documenting the paper or concept, setup instructions, implementation details, experiments, and findings.

## Collaboration

We believe the best way to understand research is to learn together, ask better questions, and test ideas in practice.
